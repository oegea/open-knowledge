# ADR 0016 — Timed transcripts for narrated audio/video materials

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-17

## Context

Courses increasingly ship narrated chapters: an `audio` material whose notes
are the full text being read aloud. Playing the audio while reading is a
richer way to study — but only if the reader can tell *where* the narration
is. Two things were missing:

1. The text did not react to playback. Readers had to find their place by
   ear, and lost it as soon as they looked away.
2. The player lived at the top of the material. After scrolling into the
   text it disappeared, together with any sense of how far along the
   narration was or how to pause it.

Speech tooling makes per-word timings cheap to produce (text-to-speech APIs
return them; forced-alignment services derive them from an existing
recording), so the missing piece was a way to carry them with the course.

## Decision

### Format: an optional timed transcript file

A material of type `audio` or `video` MAY declare a `transcriptPath` pointing
at a JSON document — the **timed transcript**:

```json
{ "words": [{ "text": "El", "start": 0.06, "end": 0.16 }, ...] }
```

- `words` is the ordered list of narrated words with their timing in seconds
  (`text` as spoken, punctuation included).
- The field is `null`/absent for plain playback. It is meaningless (and
  rejected by the domain) on `markdown` and `exam` materials.
- The transcript is a **media file** like the audio itself: uploaded through
  the media endpoint under the `transcripts` kind in database mode, or a
  `media/...` path in a content repository in static mode (ADR 0013). It is
  never inlined in the course document — a chapter has ~2,000 words and the
  course payload must stay small.
- Exports (PDF/EPUB/Markdown) ignore it. The reading experience is complete
  without it; the transcript only enriches playback.

The domain models it as `TimedTranscript` (validation, lookup by time,
alignment) with a `TimedTranscriptRepository`; the browser fetches it lazily
through `HttpTimedTranscriptRepository` only when the material is opened.

### Alignment happens in the browser, against the rendered words

The narrated text and the displayed Markdown come from the same source but
are never byte-identical (Markdown syntax is not read aloud, an image is
skipped, a sentence may be cut). Rather than demanding a perfect match, the
study view:

- renders the notes with every word wrapped in a numbered, React-owned span
  (a rehype plugin — the DOM is never mutated behind React's back);
- aligns the transcript to those spans with a tolerant, monotonic
  left-to-right match on normalized tokens (case and punctuation ignored,
  small gaps on either side skipped);
- degrades silently to plain playback when fewer than 60% of the narrated
  words find their displayed twin — a wrong transcript must never break a
  course.

### Interaction

- The current word is highlighted (`aria-current`) and kept inside a
  comfortable viewport band; the page scrolls smoothly only when the word
  would leave that band, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Any reader-initiated scroll (wheel, touch, keyboard) pauses following; a
  floating "Follow the narration" chip resumes it. Tapping a narrated word
  seeks the media there.
- While the full player is scrolled out of view, a compact **mini player**
  docks into the sticky study header (play/pause, title, elapsed/total,
  seek) — the reader never loses the controls or the position. It exists for
  every audio/video material, transcript or not.

### Sticky header fix

`html, body { overflow-x: hidden }` turned `<body>` into a scroll container
and silently disabled every `position: sticky` in the page (the study
header and the desktop contents rail never actually stuck). It is now
`overflow-x: clip`, which clips without creating a scroll container.

## Consequences

- Narrated courses become an audiobook-like experience on phones without
  any new runtime dependency; plain courses are untouched.
- Producing a transcript is a content concern (any TTS/alignment tool); the
  format is small enough to be generated by hand or by AI tooling
  (ADR 0014) and is documented in the content repository scaffold.
- Content hosts must allow cross-origin reads of the transcript JSON (GitHub
  raw URLs do); the audio itself has no such requirement.
- The material schema grows by one optional, backward-compatible field
  (ADR 0006): a nullable `transcript_path` column added with
  `addColumnIfMissing`, and a `transcripts` media kind restricted to JSON.
